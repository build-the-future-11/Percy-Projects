## Algebra, completeness and the rectangle

Write `theta³=3theta+1`, `eta=theta+1`, `delta=theta−1`, and use the ordered
embeddings `theta`, `−(theta+1)/theta`, `−1/(theta+1)`. With
`a=log(eta/theta)`, `b=log(theta)`, `C=a+b`, `d=−log(delta)` and
`R=a²+ab+b²`, the unit-log lattice has matrix

```text
L = [[a, −b], [−C, −a]],  L² = R I,  |det L| = R.
```

The following dependencies were checked in the manuscripts and independently
calculated where finite arithmetic is involved:

- The polynomial discriminant is 81. An index of 3 or 9 would give a totally real
  cubic field discriminant 9 or 1, contrary to the standard Minkowski lower bound.
  Consequently the integral basis is `1, theta, theta²`. This use of Minkowski is
  an analytical prerequisite, not something the new script proves.
- The initial unit cover uses the subgroup generated by `theta` and `eta`; it
  does not assume that these already generate the full unit group. This avoids
  circular reasoning in the later finite unit enumeration.
- The unit cover and product formula bound an optimizer's absolute norm below 9.
  Root-free cubic reductions modulo 2, 5 and 7 prove inertness at those primes;
  divisibility of a principal ideal's norm then excludes 2, 4, 5, 6 and 7. Norm 8
  is separately excluded because division by 2 produces a feasible unit with half
  the objective. Retaining that norm-8 argument is necessary.
- The exact identity `delta³=3 theta eta⁻²` and absolute norm 3 identify the unique
  prime above 3. Every absolute-norm-3 element is therefore `delta` times a unit.
- For a reduced unit in the fundamental parallelogram, the interpolation bounds
  imply `|P|<5`, `|Q|<4`, `|S|<3`. Exhausting all 314 nonzero integer triples
  gives exactly 42 signed units; each is explicitly expressed using `theta` and
  `eta`. Reduction of any unit to that box now proves full generation.
- The independent 384-case calculation serves a different purpose: it establishes
  an open region where a nonunit strictly beats every unit. The interpolation
  caps give `|P|<3`, `|Q|<6`, `|S|<4`; exact quadratic extrema over root intervals
  exclude 368 then 14 triples. The remaining pair is `±(1,5,3)`, with absolute
  norm 3. Counting only the displayed survivors would not prove completeness.

In coordinates `p=−z1−z2`, `q=(z1−z2)/2`, the strict winning set for `delta`
is represented by

```text
−d ≤ p < 0,       (b−d)/2 < q < (b+d)/2.
```

The left edge is feasible, the right edge admits unit 1, and the two horizontal
edges are ties; the half-open and strict inequalities matter. Necessity follows
by comparison with the two explicit units in the area note. For sufficiency,
translation by `eta` would send any competing unit into the empty triangle
`x<a`, `y<0`, `x+y>−C`. Applying `L⁻¹` reduces its possible integral lattice
coordinates to four points, each violating a strict inequality. Since every
nonunit optimizer is in the `delta` unit coset, projection of this rectangle is
the entire strict nonunit-winning event, not merely a positive-measure subset.

The `(z1,z2)` to `(p,q)` transformation has determinant of absolute value one.
The rectangle has area `d²`. Its translated four corners lie strictly inside
`L[0,1]²`; the verifier encloses all four corner coordinates in `(0,1)` using
outward rational intervals. Convexity gives containment and injectivity of its
projection. Dividing by covolume `R` therefore gives normalized area `rho=d²/R`.
No material gap was found in this dependency chain in this internal review;
the analytical deductions are not machine checked.

## Why the three sampling questions differ

The flow direction is
`omega=((b−a)/(2R), (2a+b)/(2R))`. Rational independence of `a,b` follows by
exponentiating a putative integer relation to an algebraic unit identity, then
using a conjugate identity. The resulting two linear equations have nonzero
determinant `R`. This establishes `k·omega != 0` for nonzero integral `k` and
continuous-flow equidistribution. It does not establish `k·omega` is nonintegral.

For a finite polygonal event, the ordinary-height note controls variation along
unit-length pieces of the lifted flow. Sum–integral comparison gives

```text
A_N = G(x0 + omega log N) + O((1+log N)/N),
G(x) = integral_0^infinity exp(−s) f(x−s omega) ds.
```

The exponential convolution has nonvanishing Fourier multiplier
`1/(1+2 pi i k·omega)`, so a nonconstant event yields a nonconstant `G`.
Dense continuous-flow tails and rounding `exp(t)` to integers give distinct
subsequential limits at continuity points. Boundary-aligned trajectories require
the manuscript's finite-stratum convention; arbitrary pathological values on an
edge would invalidate the bounded-variation argument. The Math11 rectangle has
the required regularity. Weighting by `1/n` instead gives the continuous-flow
average, so the harmonic density is `rho`. An alternative open-winning/open-losing
interval argument also rules out ordinary natural density for Math11.

For step one, the missing assertion is that `1, omega1, omega2` are rationally
independent, equivalently that `a,b,R` are rationally independent. A hypothetical
relation `R=r a+t b` is quadratic in logarithms; the preceding conjugation
argument and linear-form theorems cannot simply be applied to this real identity.

The fixed-step note treats both possible orbit closures. Any resonance subgroup
has rank at most one. If generated by `kappa`, with integer `m=kappa·omega`,
then `m != 0`; also `beta=kappa·((a+b)/R, −b/R) != 0`. These two nonzero
coefficients ensure the orbit's Haar measure gives the rectangle boundary zero
mass. The limiting frequency exists even in the resonant case. A periodized
convolution of two uniform intervals gives

```text
|F_s−rho| ≤ d / (R max(|m|,|beta|)).
```

The inverse coordinate identities bound
`||kappa||_infinity ≤ (a+3b/2) max(|m|,|beta|)`. Excluding all characters of
supremum norm at most `N` therefore bounds every phase's limiting frequency by
`rho ± d(a+3b/2)/(R(N+1))`. The executable certificate verifies `N=1000`.
This reasoning gives no finite-sample convergence rate and no exclusion outside
that finite box. Neither stronger statement is used in the new unit.

## Primary related work and novelty boundary

- Lemmermeyer and Pethö, *Simplest Cubic Number Fields*, Manuscripta Mathematica 88
  (1995), 53–58, [author-deposited full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1202.6022).
  Its introduction and proof use this classical cubic family, log-unit reduction,
  coefficient bounds and small-norm enumeration. These are established methods,
  not fresh novelty claims for Math11. Its stated parameter and squarefreeness
  hypotheses must not be silently specialized to the discriminant-81 case.
- Tao, *254B, Notes 1* (2010), [author's exposition](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2010/03/28/254b-notes-1-equidistribution-of-polynomial-sequences-in-torii/),
  Proposition 1 and the linear case. The character criterion supports the
  distinction between asymptotic equidistribution and quantitative finite-scale
  bounds. The new note's finite exclusion does not by itself supply the latter.
- Roy and Waldschmidt, *Quadratic Relations between Logarithms of Algebraic
  Numbers*, Proc. Japan Acad. 71A (1995), 151–153,
  [author-hosted paper](https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~michel.waldschmidt/articles/pdf/ProjectEuclid/ProcJapanAcad71-1995.pdf),
  Theorem 2. This is a conditional theorem with a transcendence-degree hypothesis.
  The fixed-step manuscript's further use of it to analyze a hypothetical
  resonant phase dependence is outside this unit's verified analytical scope;
  it is unnecessary for the area or uniform limiting-frequency enclosure.
